# Healthcare quality brief: diabetes care-gap monitoring, NHANES 2017-2018

Generated by `nhanes-diabetes report` from `outputs/tables` on 2026-09-19. Every number below is read from a generated table. None is typed by hand.

This is care-gap monitoring on public survey data: a measure-inspired look at who meets the laboratory criterion for diabetes but has not been told they have it. It is not a certified HEDIS measure, it uses no protected health information, and it is not clinical guidance.

## Decision question

If a health system builds a diabetes screening model or plans screening outreach, does labelling people by "a doctor told them" versus "their HbA1c meets the ADA criterion" change who is missed and who is over-flagged, and where does the undiagnosed gap concentrate?

## Population and exclusions

5,873 of 9,254 NHANES 2017-2018 participants form the analysis cohort. It represents the US civilian non-institutionalized population aged 12 and older with an exam weight, a valid diabetes questionnaire answer, a measured HbA1c and a known race and ethnicity group. ## Three computed findings, with uncertainty

1. **Undiagnosed diabetes gap.** Of 711 respondents meeting the HbA1c criterion (22.5 million people once weighted), 18.9% (15.5% to 22.8%) were never told they have diabetes. Non-Hispanic Black respondents: 32.2% (23.0% to 43.1%; n = 181, effective n = 141). Non-Hispanic White respondents: 12.8% (7.5% to 21.1%; n = 203, effective n = 105). Difference +19.4 pp (95% CI +7.0 to +31.8 pp, p = 0.004), ratio 2.51 (95% CI 1.34 to 4.69). This is the one pre-specified comparison.
2. **With race excluded, the training label barely changes the primary age-and-sex model.** AUC against the HbA1c criterion is 0.758 for the diagnosed label and 0.756 for the HbA1c label (difference +0.001, 95% CI 0.000 to +0.003, bootstrap p = 0.156). Overall specificity cost +0.7 pp (95% CI -0.3 to +1.8 pp).
3. **In this comparison the label matters when race is a model input.** With race included, the specificity cost is +10.0 pp for Non-Hispanic Black respondents (95% CI +8.6 to +11.2 pp) and -1.9 pp for Non-Hispanic White respondents (95% CI -3.7 to -0.1 pp).

## What this means for screening outreach

- **Size of the gap.** An estimated 4.2 million people (18.9%, 95% CI 15.5% to 22.8%) of the 22.5 million who meet the HbA1c criterion had not been told they have diabetes.
- **Where to look first.** Among groups without a small-sample warning, the estimated undiagnosed share is highest for Non-Hispanic Black (32.2%, 23.0% to 43.1%) and lowest for Non-Hispanic White (12.8%, 7.5% to 21.1%). Intervals for most groups overlap, and only the Black versus White comparison was named in advance, so the other groups are a prompt for monitoring, not a ranking.
- **What outreach would need.** Monitoring screening reach and follow-up in groups with a higher estimated undiagnosed share, using local data and the same definitions, is the supported next step. Roughly 4.2 million is a national estimate, not a local target.
- **Cost of the label choice.** With race excluded, the two labels give nearly the same ranking (AUC 0.758 and 0.756). With race included, training on the HbA1c label moves the false-positive rate for Non-Hispanic Black respondents from 34.6% to 44.6% (+10.0 pp, 95% CI +8.6 to +11.2 pp), which is extra outreach workload for people without the condition. That is a reason to quantify both labels by group before swapping one for the other.

## What the data cannot establish

- **Why the gap exists.** Access to care, screening frequency, insurance, clinician behavior and measurement can all produce it, and this survey cannot separate them. Nothing here shows clinician bias.
- **A causal effect of anything.** The estimates are descriptive and cross-sectional.
- **Local rates.** The estimates describe the US civilian non-institutionalized population, not a health system's patients.
- **Clinical correctness of either label.** HbA1c is one of three ADA criteria, fasting glucose is not used, and HbA1c can read high in some people with sickle cell trait.
- **Readiness of any model.** The models are minimal comparisons of two labels, not clinical risk models, and none has been validated for deployment.
- **Anything about a specific person.** No respondent-level result is reported.

## Recommended next analysis

1. Repeat the estimate with the same definitions on the next NHANES cycle to see whether the gap persists.
2. Run the same definitions on local EHR or claims data, with a governed extract, to size the gap for the health system's own population and to test whether the HbA1c label behaves the same way.
3. Add fasting glucose as a second criterion, with the fasting-subsample weight, to test how much of the gap depends on using HbA1c alone.
4. Before any screening workflow changes, pair the false-positive burden with local follow-up capacity so that the cost of over-flagging is known.

## Metric glossary

| Metric | Definition | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Undiagnosed share | Weighted share of people meeting the HbA1c criterion who were never told by a doctor that they have diabetes. | Proportion, shown as a percentage. Interval is a 95% logit interval on design degrees of freedom. |
| People with undiagnosed diabetes, weighted | Survey-weighted count of people meeting the HbA1c criterion who were never told they have diabetes. | People represented by the survey weights. No interval is reported. |
| Undiagnosed share difference versus reference group | Undiagnosed share of a group minus the undiagnosed share of the reference group (Non-Hispanic White). | Percentage points, shown as a percentage. Only the Non-Hispanic Black comparison is confirmatory; the others are exploratory with Holm-adjusted p-values. |
| Effective sample size | Kish effective sample size of the HbA1c-positive respondents in a group, which shrinks when weights are unequal. | Respondents. A group with fewer than 30 respondents or an effective n under 30 carries a small-sample warning. |
| AUC against the HbA1c criterion | Weighted area under the ROC curve of out-of-fold predictions, scored against the HbA1c criterion. | Area under the curve, 0.5 to 1. Interval is a Rao-Wu rescaled PSU bootstrap. |
| AUC difference between labels | AUC of the model trained on the diagnosed label minus AUC of the model trained on the HbA1c label, both scored against the HbA1c criterion. | AUC points. Interval and p-value come from a paired bootstrap. |
| Sensitivity | Weighted share of HbA1c-positive respondents the model flags at its cross-validated threshold. | Proportion, shown as a percentage. |
| Specificity | Weighted share of HbA1c-negative respondents the model does not flag. | Proportion, shown as a percentage. |
| False-positive rate | One minus specificity. The share of HbA1c-negative respondents the model would flag for follow-up. | Proportion, shown as a percentage. Computed as a live formula in the workbook. |
| Specificity cost of the HbA1c label | Specificity of the model trained on the diagnosed label minus specificity of the model trained on the HbA1c label. Positive means more false positives when training on the HbA1c label. | Percentage points, shown as a percentage. Interval is a paired bootstrap. |
| Holm-adjusted p-value | p-value of a group comparison after Holm adjustment across the five comparisons with the reference group. | Probability. A group comparison is labelled exploratory unless it is the pre-specified contrast. |
| Missingness share | Share of respondents in a group with no valid value for an input variable. | Proportion, shown as a percentage. Computed as a live formula in the workbook. |
